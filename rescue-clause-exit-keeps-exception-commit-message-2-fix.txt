A break, next or return inside a rescue clause keeps the clause's exception

    begin
      raise ArgumentError, "low"
    rescue ArgumentError
      [1, 2].each { |x| next }
      p $!
      raise KeyError, "high"
    end

printed nil, and the KeyError had no cause. The `next` popped the
ArgumentError off the handled stack, and the clause's own pop at its end
then took sp_rescue_sp below zero: the caller's `$!` was lost too, and a
few such clauses in a row ended in a segmentation fault.

An exit pops the rescue bodies it leaves, and it told them by frame
depth: those at or above the depth of its target. But a rescue clause
runs with its frame already popped, so a loop, an ensure region, an
inlined method or a break wrapper opened inside the clause stands at the
clause's own depth, and an exit to it counted the clause it never left.

Each rescue body now records what was open at its entry (the loop depth,
the ensure depth, the return funnel), and an exit to a loop, an ensure
region or an inlined method's funnel counts the bodies begun inside its
target. A deferred return, next or break pops at the deferral only the
bodies inside the region, and each ensure tail pops the ones between it
and the next target, so the ensure body still reads the clause's `$!`.
A break wrapper puts sp_rescue_sp back at its landing, as it does the
other depths: a thrown break passes frames that restore their own mark,
so no count at the break could be right. Only a wrapper whose body
handles an exception takes that snapshot.

A rescue modifier's fallback is registered as a rescue body as well. It
never was, so `x rescue next` left its exception handled, and inside a
rescue clause that leak and the wrong pop had cancelled out.
