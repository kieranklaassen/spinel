An append chained on a safe-navigation append runs that link once

    def f
      t = +"4"
      t << "2"
      t&.<<("a") << "b"
      t
    end
    p f

printed "42aab"; it is "42ab". A statement whose receiver is a `&.`
call tests that call's answer for nil, so emit_nil_target_stmt runs the
link into a temp ahead of the statement. str_mutate_append_bang_arms
then walked the chain of appends down to the local, through that link
again, and appended its operand a second time. An operand that is a
call ran twice: `t&.<<((n += 1).to_s) << "b"` left n at 2.

The walk now gathers no link the statement's head ran already
(head_held), nor a link under it: those appended there, and the walk
only goes on to the receiver. A chain with no `&.` link keeps its C,
and so does a chain on a String local that is not a handle, where the
link's first run goes to its temp and the walk's is the one that
counts.
