A boxed div, divmod, remainder or fdiv raises for an operand that is no number

Each of the four refuses a receiver without the method and then
converts its operand: nil to 0, true to 1, a Symbol to its index, a
String through strtoll or Float().

    row = [7, true]
    p row[0].divmod(row[1])    # Spinel: [7, 0], CRuby: TypeError

    row = [7, nil]
    p row[0].div(row[1])       # Spinel: ZeroDivisionError, CRuby: TypeError

`2.5.fdiv("s")` raised ArgumentError, and the Float arms worded the
TypeError as a conversion ("can't convert nil into Float") where CRuby
words it as a coercion.

Below its receiver guard each now sends an operand outside the numeric
tower to sp_poly_binop_bad, which raises "nil can't be coerced into
Integer" (Float, Rational, by the receiver). A program object with
coerce has answered above, in sp_poly_coerce_binop. Nothing is removed
and no arm changes; sp_poly_divmod's Float arm is the one that calls
sp_flo_divmod. In div, divmod and remainder an Integer, a Float or a
Bignum operand passes on its tag and a Rational on its kind; the rest
of the test is out of line (sp_poly_operand_bad). fdiv reads those four
kinds of operand itself and asks the check only for what is left.

A program whose builtin classes have arithmetic or a coerce of their
own is left as it is, as for the boxed / and %: the helpers do not ask
a reopened TrueClass#coerce, and the conversion often answers what it
does (`class TrueClass; def coerce(n) = [n, 1]; end` and
`7.divmod(true)`: [7, 0] on CRuby and, by the conversion, here). The
four checks read sp_poly_divmod_converts after the operand has failed
them.

Left alone: `**`, which converts the same way in sp_poly_pow; a
receiver the compiler knows to be an Integer or a Float, which does not
come through these functions.
