An append through delegating readers looks each method name up once

A bus of 512 classes that hand one another's `peek` on
(`def peek(i) = @nxt.peek(i)`), with 512 lines `bus.peek(k) << "!"`,
compiled in 4.5 seconds before pull request 7806 ("A method reached
again from nearer the top follows its results") and takes 39 since.
Twice the bus took 3.9 times as long before it and takes 7.9 times as
long now.

That change is right and what it does stays: a method first met at the
walk's bound is walked again when it is reached from nearer the top.
But a receiver of no known class goes by name. The first descent takes
nearly every class's `peek` to the bound, each is then walked again at
every depth above it, and each of those walks looked through all the
scopes for the methods of its result, to find them walked. A demand
made about four times N such scans where it had made four.

strbuf_demand_user_elem_call now keeps, for a demand, the depth at
which each name's by-name loop was begun. Such a loop takes every
method of the name to its own depth or nearer the top, so a call of
the name no nearer the top than one begun, ended or still running, has
nothing to add and returns. A receiver of a known class has one method
of the name; it is looked up once a call instead of once a scope, which
takes back half of what the same change added for one class whose
methods reach one another by several routes (1,024 methods, five
routes each: 7.2 seconds before, 20.7 on master, 11.5 with this). The
rest is that change's own five visits a method.

No generated C changes. make scale-test gains the bus at 64 and 128
classes (test/scale/reader_bus.sh): the work at twice the bus is 2.56
times before that change, 4.35 on master and 2.59 with this.
