uniq with a block keeps an Enumerator's items, not what the block binds

    e = [5, 6, 5].each_with_index
    p e.uniq { |x| x }

printed [5, 6] for [[5, 0], [6, 1]]. The other way round, over
o = [5, 6, 5, 7].each, o.uniq { |*r| r } printed [[5], [6], [7]] for
[5, 6, 7] and o.uniq(&:odd?) printed [[5], [6]] for [5, 6].

The to_a that stands in front of a block iterator over an Enumerator
hands each item as the block binds it (enum_hop_yield_view): the first
value a step yields for a lone |x|, all of them for a lone |*r| or
&:m. That is right for map or count, whose answer is the block's. uniq
answers the items, and got what the block binds in their place.

emit_poly_uniq_block now reads the Enumerator itself under such a
view. For a lone |x| the block binds the first values, as before, and
a step that packed two values or more has a new Array of its values
for its survivor, made when the survivor is pushed. For a lone
|*r| or &:m the view's to_a stands as it did, a new Array of each
step's values, and that Array is the survivor, as on master, but for a
step of one value nothing can change in place (an Integer, a Float, a
Symbol, nil, true or false): that value is the step's survivor. Any
other one value keeps its Array for the survivor: it may be a String
or an Array the Enumerator made and keeps (each_char, each_slice), and
what uniq answers must not be an object the Enumerator's next to_a
hands out again. So the Array a step packed is never the survivor
either: the survivor is a new one of the same values.

What that Array is made of depends on what can run between the first
step and the last push. An Enumerator hands out its own items, so a
block that calls anything, or an == of the program's own, may reach
them through another to_a while the steps run, and master reads the
first values ahead. Where nothing of the program's runs there (the
block's body is reads of its parameter and literals, and the program
as written gives no class an ==, an eql?, a hash or a <=>), the Array
is made of the item itself: an Enumerator that keeps its items is
read by the view's own to_a, so the step loop is master's, and the
item is the one it keeps; a generator, which makes its items anew at
every to_a, is read once, plain, with the first values taken beside.
For any other block the first values go in place ahead of the first
step and the values after the first are kept beside them, one step's
after another's. No call is on the list: the parameter is the step's
first value, of any class, and a Hash's [] runs its default block.

Under a lone |*r| the one value is asked of a survivor only, when it
is pushed, where the block leaves its Array as it is: its body uses
the parameter only as the receiver of an index that is not assigned,
of size, length, first or last, each of them the builtin's (the
program gives no class a method of that name), which is all the body
written for &:m does. Any other block may change its Array
({ |*q| k = q[0]; q.pop; k }), so there the value is taken ahead of
the block.

It does so only where the program as written gives no class a uniq of
its own (an_prog_never_gives): no def and no Symbol of that name, and
no site where a method is named, made or loaded by something the text
does not spell. A uniq the program gives Enumerable or Enumerator, in
a file the compiler reads or in one it does not, answers what it
likes, so such a program keeps master's C.

A step that yields one value has the parameter for its item, and a
block may change that String in place: `x.chomp!` rebinds the
parameter, and uniq kept the changed one. So for a lone |x| the
survivor of such a step is the parameter as it is after the block
ran, as it was.

By callgrind, instructions a step with gcc and with clang, on programs
that size the answer, which master runs right. A lone |x| over steps
of one value (a.each, each_char, each_slice(2)) stays within 2 of
master or under it. Over steps of several values, { |x| x } is 4
fewer and 15 more over each_with_index (of 929 and 885) and 1 and 25
more over a generator of two or of three values a step; clang's 15
are sp_yielded_packed, inlined at its one call on master and called
here, where it has two. A block that calls anything ({ |x| x + 0 })
pays for the values kept ahead: 88 and 104 over each_with_index, 44
and 49 over the generator of two values, 84 and 89 of three. A lone
|*r| that only reads its Array, and &:abs, stay within 1 of master
over each_with_index, over that generator and over a.each; one that
may change it pays 15 and 9 over the generator and 23 and 26 over
a.each. spinel -c of a program of 1,000 and of 2,000 such calls,
against master: 3.83% and 8.07% fewer for the lone |x|, 3.28% and
4.89% fewer for the lone |*r|, and the same to 0.03% over an Array,
whose C is master's. A block with two parameters, a block with none
and no block keep their C.
