A `&.` call to a builtins/ scalar method takes a boxed argument

    def id(x) = x
    id(:s)
    def flt(v) = v ? 1.5 : nil
    f = flt(true)
    p f&.clamp(1.0, id(1.2))    # 1.2

This raises NoMethodError ("undefined method 'clamp' for an instance of
Float"); `i&.between?(id(1), id(20))` does not build, and
`i&.remainder(id(5.0))` answers 2 for 2.0. All three were right before
"v&.fdiv(2) and the other Ruby-defined Integer methods keep the &.":
that left every `&.` call on the typed emitter, which does not serve a
boxed argument.

With a boxed argument the call is moved onto its builtins/ definition
after all, as `(t = recv; t.nil? ? nil : copy(t, args))`, the shape the
Enumerable move gives a `&.` call. Any other `&.` call stays on the
typed emitter.
