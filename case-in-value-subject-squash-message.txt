A by-value case/in subject keeps its Strings while it is deconstructed

A small class that is never written is kept by value: the struct lives
in the C temp emit_case_match binds its subject to. For a subject made
in place (`case mk(i)`, `mk(i) => [q, t]`) that temp is the only holder
of the object's String fields. The match rooted its subject with
emit_gc_root_tmp, which takes nothing for a by-value object, so the
String was collected when #deconstruct or #deconstruct_keys allocated
before it read it, and the pattern bound a freed String: of 3,000
matches, 5 bind another String in a plain run.

The subject is now rooted with emit_gc_root_tmp_refs, which roots a
by-value object's String fields one by one, the way a local of that
class is rooted, and anything else as emit_gc_root_tmp did. `=>` and
`in` as a condition are case/in by the time they are emitted and take
the same line.

test/case_in_value_subject_string_root.rb counts the wrong matches of
3,000 through nine forms. On master every line is wrong in a plain
run.
