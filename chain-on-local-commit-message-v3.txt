A chain of in-place String methods on a local changes it at every link

    s = +"c"
    s.prepend("b").prepend("a")
    p s

printed "bc", where CRuby prints "abc". `s.concat("a").concat("b")` left
"a", `s.clear.concat("z")` left "", and `s.replace("r").concat("s")`
left "r". Only a chain in which every link is `<<` kept every change.

`s = +""; s.reverse!.concat("x").clear` left "x": the middle link is
written back after a bang, and the last one changed a temporary.

A String mutator is lowered to a write of its receiver, and only a
receiver that is a name has a place to write. The second link's receiver
is the first link's value, a temporary, so its change went there and s
kept the first one alone.

A new pass, desugar_mutator_chain_on_local, makes the links calls on the
local, in order:

    s.m(x).n(y).o(z)  ->  (s.m(x); s.n(y); s.o(z))

for links m and n that always answer their receiver: `<<` and concat
with one argument, prepend, clear, reverse!, freeze, force_encoding, and
replace by a String literal. Each argument runs once and in its order.

Only a chain whose value nothing reads is rewritten: a statement that is
not its sequence's last, or the last of the program, of a loop's body,
of a begin, a paren or an arm of a conditional that is itself dropped,
or of the block of `loop` or of a loop method on an Array, a Hash, a
Range or an Integer that the program neither defines nor names in a
Symbol. An Enumerator is typed as an Array when this pass runs, so the
block of its `each` counts too; `y.yield` answers nil there today.
Where a write takes a sequence's value it takes a copy, so a
chain whose value is assigned, passed, compared, returned or sent
another call stays whole. What reads a value is asked of the parents the
program's tree reaches (du_parent_map).

Each link becomes the statement `s.m(x)`, so the rewrite is made only
where that statement does what the link did. Left as they were, each
for what its statement does today:

- a link or a last call the program defines a method named as, in any
  class or by a Symbol: the statement runs the builtin;
- a local a lambda, a proc or a kept block reads, or any block writes:
  two of the statements on a captured local do not build;
- a local that may be nil, where the chain raises NoMethodError and the
  statements crash. The nil facts are made after this pass, so it asks
  for less: every write of the name in its method is a String made on
  the spot (a literal, `+` or dup of one, String.new) and one of them
  is a statement ahead of the chain. A parameter is not such a local;
- a local that is also a target, or written with an operator: `s += x`
  beside a statement's append is refused;
- an insert, as a link or as the last call, and an index assignment as
  the last call: their statements do not root what they are given;
- a replace by anything but a literal: its statement leaves the local
  naming a temporary. A chain that ends with a replace;
- the call after a link that may answer nil (`s.upcase!.concat("z")`);
- encode!, a concat with another count of arguments, a chain of `<<`
  alone (it has its own lowering), a link with a block, a `&.`, or an
  argument that writes a variable; anything but a String local.
