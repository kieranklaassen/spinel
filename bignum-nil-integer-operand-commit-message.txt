A Bignum operation whose Integer operand is nil answers as CRuby does

An Integer local, instance variable or method result that can be nil
holds the nil as a sentinel. Where a Bignum operation takes such an
operand, emit_bigint_operand makes the nil the NULL a Bignum slot
holds, and the operation read through it: `2**70 + n`, `2**70 == n`,
`2**70 < n`, `n * 2**70`, `(2**70).div(n)` and `@big += n` died with
signal 11 for an `n` that was nil.

Now the operand is kept as it is read and converted as the number it
holds, so the operation runs with its operands in the order they ran
in, and the nil is answered after it by the runtime's own tests
(SP_INT_NIL_CK, SP_INT_NIL_CMP_CK): `==` false and `!=` true, an
ordering compare ArgumentError, arithmetic TypeError, and
NoMethodError where the nil is the receiver. modulo, div, divmod,
pow(a, m), quo, allbits?, anybits? and nobits? raise their TypeError.
Where neither operand can hold nil the C is unchanged.
