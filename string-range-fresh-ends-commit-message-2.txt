A String Range is two Strings by value. A local's two ends are rooted and
an instance variable's are marked, but a temp's were not. So a Range that
was a method's value, an argument on its way to a callee, the receiver of
cover? or one side of == lost its ends to the next allocation: the
membership argument, the callee's first String, the other side. A
global's Range read as a receiver is such a temp too, and the argument
that binds the global to another Range let its ends go.

The two ends of a String Range temp are now rooted where the temp is
bound, with emit_gc_root_tmp_refs as the poly dispatch does: the operand
temps, an argument's temp, the local of an inlined method, the receiver of
include?, member?, cover?, === and eql? when the argument may allocate,
and each side of == and != beside a side that may allocate.

Rooting a Range takes it whole, and one written with a read beside an end
that runs something (`(s..(s = t; j.to_s))`), or with two ends each read
from a String that was appended to, could already carry a freed end. Such
an end now goes into a rooted slot where the C compiler makes it, in the
order the C compiler picks. A Range whose ends are literals or plain reads
compiles to the C it did.

The test joins GC_STRESS_TESTS: without this it aborts at level 2 ahead of
its last five lines.

Not here: a String Range passed to a Proc, taken apart by a multiple
assignment, read by inspect or out of a Struct member still loses an end
at level 2.
