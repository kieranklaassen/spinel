slice!(re, n) holds the head of the receiver while it cuts the tail

slice! with a Regexp and a group rebuilds the receiver from what is left
of it. The arm wrote that as one C expression,
sp_str_concat(sp_str_byteslice(..), sp_str_byteslice(..)): whichever
piece C built first was held by nothing while the other was allocated,
since sp_str_concat roots its parameters only once entered. A collection
between the two freed the first piece, and the receiver came back with
other bytes in its place:

    bad = 0
    i = 0
    while i < 4000
      n = 700 + (i * 37) % 900
      s = +("a" * n + "ll" + "b" * n)
      r = s.slice!(/(l)(l)/, 2)
      bad += 1 unless s.count("a") == n && s.count("b") == n
      i += 1
    end
    p bad

printed 2 in a plain run, 1191 with SPINEL_GC_STRESS=1, and stopped with
"the mark reached a freed heap string" at level 2; CRuby prints 0.

The head now goes into a rooted temp before the tail is cut, as the
index assignment by a group does beside it.

Test: test/string_slice_bang_group_rooted.rb, also in GC_STRESS_TESTS.
