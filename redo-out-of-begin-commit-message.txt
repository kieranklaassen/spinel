A redo out of a begin or a rescue clause pops its frame and its exception

    def read(items)
      n = 0
      items.each do |x|
        n += 1
        begin
          raise IOError, "again" if n == 1
        rescue IOError
          redo
        end
      end
      $!
    end

    p read([1, 2])

printed #<IOError: again> where CRuby prints nil, and the 65th call
stopped the program with "rescue nesting too deep". With the redo in
the begin's body instead, the 64th call ended in a SystemStackError.

A redo is a goto back to the label at the top of its body. It popped
nothing: a begin written around it kept its setjmp frame on the
handler stack, and a rescue clause around it kept its exception as the
one being handled, one more at every redo.

The label now keeps what stands open where it is placed (frames,
ensure regions, rescue clauses), and a redo pops the frames and the
clauses above it, as a next does for its loop. A redo with nothing
between it and its body is the goto it was. So is one with an ensure
in between: that ensure has to run first, which this does not take on.
