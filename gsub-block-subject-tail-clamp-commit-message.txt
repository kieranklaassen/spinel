sub with a block copies no tail when the match ends past the subject

    x = ""
    p x.sub("\0") { "" }.bytes

aborts in the allocator ("malloc(): corrupted top size") after the
commit before this one; CRuby and master print [].

That commit gives the tail copy its length, the subject's length less
the match's end. A String pattern is sought by strstr, which reads
it to its first NUL, while the match's end is counted by the pattern's
byte length. With a NUL in the pattern the end can lie past the
subject's end: the length came out negative and the copy ran over the
heap.

sub's tail copy is now emitted under a test that the end lies inside
the subject, so such a match has no tail, as before. gsub's own tail
line is already under its loop's bound.

A String pattern is still cut at its first NUL:
`"ab".sub("b\0") { "q" }` answers "aq" where CRuby finds no match, as it
did.
