A Regexp's tenth group and beyond are read by number

A match keeps the Strings of nine groups (sp_re_captures) and the
positions of thirty-two (sp_re_caps), and every read by number asked
the Strings, with a bound of 9:

    "abcdefghijk" =~ /(a)(b)(c)(d)(e)(f)(g)(h)(i)(j)(k)/
    p $9, $10, $~[11], Regexp.last_match(10), $~[-1]

printed "i", nil, nil, nil, nil; CRuby prints "i", "j", "k", "j", "k".
defined?($10) and s[re, 10] answered nil the same way.

sp_re_group answers a group by number: the kept String for 1 to 9, and
past the ninth the subject cut at the kept position, which is how $`
and $' are built already. A negative number counts back from the
pattern's last group.

The positions are the match's own only where the match filled
sp_re_caps. String#match and Regexp#match run the engine into a caps
array of their own and hand $~ their Strings alone, so one int,
sp_re_caps_own, says which it was, and a group past the ninth is cut
only where it holds; elsewhere it reads nil as before. A method's frame
saves the int in a spare bit of the flag beside it: the frame is on the
C stack and stays the 400 bytes it was. The collector's marks stay as
they are.

$N and Regexp.last_match(N) with a literal N of 1 to 9 read
sp_re_captures[N] as before. The reads by a number known only at run
time ($~[n], Regexp.last_match(n), s[re, n]) go through one emitter,
emit_re_group_read, so the bound is written once.

slice!(re, n) and s[re, n] = v keep their bound of 9: they move text by
the same positions and are a change of their own.

Tests: test/regexp_group_past_ninth.rb, and
test/regexp_group_past_ninth_kept_match.rb, a program left alone.
