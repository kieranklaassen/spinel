The minimum of a String Range with an excluded end is decided by its ends

sp_srange_min_v walked a range whose end is excluded and took its least
member, so ("9"..."11"), which holds "9" and "10" though "9" > "11",
answered "10" where CRuby answers nil, ("11"..."9") answered nil for
"11", ("aaa"..."zz") nil for "aaa", and an endless ("a"...) raised.
CRuby's range_min never walks with no block and no argument: the
minimum is the begin, or nil when the begin is past the end, or at it
with the end excluded.

An excluded end is compared as the walk compared it (sp_str_cmp_bytes)
and the answer is the begin itself. An included end is compared as
strcmp compared it, and by its bytes alone past a NUL when strcmp finds
no difference, so ("a\0b".."a\0a") has no minimum. The two slow paths
are kept out of line. The maximum is not touched.

Enumerable#to_h of a boxed String Range asks the same function whether
the Range has a member, and a member is decided by the walk, not by the
ends: ("9"..."11") has two and no minimum. The walk stays as it was
under a name of its own, sp_srange_least_v, and sp_poly_to_h_m calls
that, so to_h answers what it answered.
