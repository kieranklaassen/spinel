The head and the tail of a String are held while slice! joins them

slice! with an Integer, a start and a length, or a Range rebuilds the
receiver from what is left of it. All four arms, as a statement and
with the removed part taken, wrote that as one C expression,
sp_str_concat(sp_str_sub_range(..), sp_str_sub_range(..)): whichever
piece C evaluated first was held by nothing while the other was
allocated, since sp_str_concat roots its parameters only once
entered. When both pieces are longer than one character, so that both
are allocated, SPINEL_GC_STRESS=2 stopped with "the mark reached a
freed heap string". With long pieces a plain run is wrong too: two
4,000-character pieces with the removed parts kept leave 11 of 1,000
rounds holding another text, and 121 at level 1, with gcc and clang
alike.

emit_slice_bang_rejoin now writes the join for the four arms: the
head goes into a rooted temp, then the tail is made inside the call,
as sp_str_splice_at does for its own pieces. An index held boxed
re-enters the same arms and is covered. Calling sp_str_splice_at was
measured and not taken: it makes one more allocation, 392
instructions a call against about 10 for the temp.

The test runs the long round as well, so it fails on master in the
ordinary test leg and under the stress lane. The generated C of 24
tests changes, and three of them (issue_3084,
string_slice_bang_value_rooted, string_slice_bang_empty) now pass at
level 2. Every benchmark's and optcarrot's is unchanged.
