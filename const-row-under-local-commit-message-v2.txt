A constant table's row held by a local is the row, not a copy

`T = [[1, 2], [3, 4]]; e = T[1]; e << "s"; p T[1]` printed [3, 4];
CRuby prints [3, 4, "s"]. The same with `e[0] = "s"`, `e.insert`,
`e.concat(["s"])`, with a Float, a Symbol or nil for the String, and
with the row read by `T.first`, `T.last` or `T.at(1)`.

const_array_elems_all_int_array answers that the table's rows are
Integer Arrays. The push widens the local to a general Array, and the
read then converts the row: the local held a copy, and the table kept
the row as it was.

Where the table is one literal of Integer Array literals that nothing
stores a row into, and a local bound to one of its rows is given an
element that is no Integer, the answer is now no. The rows are then
built as general Arrays, as they are when the element is given through
the table (`T[1] << "s"`), and the local reads the row itself. The
locals that hold a row and the stores made through them are listed
once a pass (row_stores_build), and the question is a scan of that
list.

The cost: such a table is read boxed whether or not the store runs.
Beside `e = T[1]; e << "s" if ARGV.size > 5`, a million rounds of
`s += T[0][i & 3] + T[i & 1][0]` go from 44,663,935 instructions to
105,675,251, about 30 a read; a walk of the rows is cheaper than it
was (`T.each { |r| t += r[0] + r[3] }`, 946 a walk to 307).

Every other table keeps its answer: one made by `map` or frozen, or
with a row stored into it, has rows the literal walk does not rebuild,
and with the answer no the push would raise a TypeError there. So
would a value of two kinds (`e << (c ? "s" : 1)`), which rebuilds no
literal. Those still hold a copy. Under --share-strings a `concat` is
left as it was too: with the rows general Arrays the flag refuses the
String literal handed to it once the table is walked.

Test: test/const_table_row_under_local.rb (expected from CRuby 3.3.6
with --enable-frozen-string-literal; master differs on 15 of its 42
lines) and, for the flag, test/share/share_strings_const_row_concat.rb,
which passes on master too.
