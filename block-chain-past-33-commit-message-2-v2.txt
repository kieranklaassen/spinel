A block handed down through more than 33 methods keeps its value

  def f1(x)
    f2(x) { |v| yield v }
  end
  ...                       # 34 methods, each one's block yielding on
  def f34(x) = yield(x + 1)
  p f1(1) { |v| v * 2 }

prints 4 in CRuby and printed nil, with nothing said. The type a
`yield` answers is read from the blocks at the method's call sites, and
where such a block's value is itself a `yield` the question goes on to
the method that block is written in. The methods being asked are kept
on a stack (g_yvt_mi), so that one handing its block to itself ends,
and the stack was an array of 32: asked 33 deep the question was
answered "unknown", the call had no type, and its value was boxed as
nil.

The stack grows. The four functions that push on it (yield_value_type,
yield_block_tails, yield_value_type_via_super, method_block_presence)
share one helper for the push (yvt_enter); a method already on the
stack still ends the walk.

The walk keeps no memory of what it has asked, so each method of a
chain asks the whole chain above it, and two kinds of method are asked
no further. A method nothing calls is asked no deeper than the 32
master asks: its yield has no block to answer for it. A method that
yields is asked only while fewer than 64 such methods are being
answered: one more would be written out 65 deep inside the others,
which the inliner refuses (SP_INLINE_DEPTH_MAX). A method with a
function of its own, such as one that calls its block as `b.call`, is
asked at any depth. So the compile takes master's time: a chain of 800,
1,600 and 3,200 methods that nothing calls compiles in 0.24, 0.58 and
1.22 s (0.27, 0.52 and 1.32 s on master), and a chain of 800 called
past the inliner's room is refused in 14.7 s (14.3 s).

Wrong on master from 34 methods and right now: the block yielded on
from a literal block and handed on as `&b`, the callee defined first,
instance methods, a String, the value kept in a local on the way up, a
block that may answer nil, two call sites answering different types,
and block_given? at the far end. They are right for as long a chain as
the inliner's rename table has room for, 64 methods of one parameter,
and from there the call is refused by name as it is on master.

test/yield_chain_long.rb has four chains of 40 methods, six of whose
seven lines are nil on master, and a chain of 65 methods that call
their block as `b.call`, which is right on master and stays so.
