The first Process::Status stays rooted while a second `$?` is read

    system("exit 3")
    p $? == $?

printed false at SPINEL_GC_STRESS=2; it is true. A read of `$?` builds
its Process::Status (sp_last_process_status allocates), and the `==`
and `!=` templates of two statuses (builtin_ops.c) bound the first to a
C temporary and then evaluated the second: that allocation collected
the first, and its status word was read from freed memory. The two
templates root the first before the second is made.

Test: test/process_status_equality.rb joins GC_STRESS_TESTS.
