A String mutator called with `&.` changes its receiver

    def str(v) = v ? +"ab" : nil
    s = str(true)
    p s&.concat("x")   # "abx"
    p s                # "abx"

printed "abx" and "ab". A String mutator is lowered to a reassignment of
its receiver, and under a `&.` guard the receiver is bound to the guard's
temp: the new String was written there and the variable kept the old
one. So did `<<`, replace, insert, upcase! and the rest where the call
was used for its value or another call followed it, and every one of
them on a boxed receiver, as a statement too.

The guard's arms now store the temp back (sn_store_back) where the
receiver is a variable read as its plain slot and the guarded call's
text assigns the temp. A call that assigns nothing keeps its C.

were wrong. Expected output from CRuby 3.3.6.
