A `&.` call on a nil receiver runs none of its arguments

    s = nil_or_string
    r = s&.rjust(lg(5), lg("b"))

ran both `lg` calls for a nil `s`; CRuby runs neither. A call to a
builtin has its operands ordered ahead of it (emit_operands_in_order),
and for a `&.` call that was ahead of the nil guard too: an argument
with an effect ran, or raised, on the very nil the guard stops.

The guard re-enters the call's emission under its nil test, so the
ordering now waits for that re-entry (sn_guard_ahead) where the guard
will run, a boxed receiver and the typed ones it tests, and where the
call's value can stay in its place (sn_stays_in_place). There the
order against the statement's other operands is kept. Where a later
operand of the statement is more than a literal or a plain read, the
call is as it was.
