A method matching by slice!, `s[re, n] = v` or `in` keeps its caller's $~

A method that matches saves its caller's $~ on the way in. The test for
"matches" knew neither slice! with a Regexp, nor an index assignment
that names a group, nor a pattern of `in` or `=>` that holds a Regexp,
so such a method left its own match in its caller's registers:

    def cut(s) = s.slice!(/(b)/)
    "q1" =~ /q(\d)/
    cut(+"abc")
    p $1

printed "b"; CRuby prints "1". So did `s[/(b)(c)/, 2] = "v"` and
`case s in /a(b)/`.

scope_performs_match now counts the three, in a program where a method's
registers are provably its own frame's (match_frame_closed). A pattern
is searched whole: the Regexp may sit in an array, a hash, an
alternative or behind a pin. `s[re] = v` sets no register and is not
counted. Every other program is emitted as before, byte for byte.

Tests: test/regexp_frame_slice_and_pattern.rb.
test/regexp_frame_slice_kept_block.rb is a program the test leaves
alone.
