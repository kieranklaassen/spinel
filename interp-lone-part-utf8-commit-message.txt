An interpolation that is one part alone starts UTF-8

    b = "a".b
    p "#{b}".encoding           # BINARY, CRuby UTF-8
    p ("#{b}" + "").encoding    # BINARY, CRuby UTF-8

An interpolation picks its encoding part by part (sp_str_enc_step),
from its first part's unless a literal leads. For "#{b}", one part
and no literal, that made the result the part's own encoding: an
ASCII-only binary String came back binary where CRuby answers UTF-8,
and keeps binary only for a part that holds a byte past ASCII
("#{"\xff".b}").

emit_interp now starts such an interpolation at UTF-8, as it starts
one a literal leads; the step then gives a part with high bytes its
own encoding, as before. An interpolation of two parts or more is not
touched: "#{b}#{b}" is UTF-8 in CRuby 3.3 and BINARY in 4.0, and
answers BINARY here as it did.
