sp_str_splice_at joins its three pieces in one allocation

A splice cut the head and the tail, joined the head and the value, and
joined that with the tail: four allocations, each piece rooted while the
next was made. The head, the value and the tail now go to one
sp_str_concat3, which roots all three once entered; the head stays
rooted while the tail is cut, and nothing allocates between the tail
and the call. The result's bytes and its binary marking are those the
two joins gave.

`s[2, 3] = "xy"` on an eight-character String took 1,327 instructions
and takes 1,145; `s[2..4] = "xy"` 1,362 and 1,180 (callgrind, 200,000
statements).
