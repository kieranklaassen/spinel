A call that declined its operands once renders them last

emit_operands_in_order renders two or more observable operands, emits
the call with them bound, and declines when the call did not take them
so; the caller then renders the whole call again, operands included. A
method written in Ruby binds its arguments in order already and
declines every time. As the receiver of another such call it is
emitted twice, once to be bound and once in that call's arm, so every
link of a chain doubled the work below it:

    def where(x) = (@w << x; self)
    q = Q.new.where(col(1)).where(col(2))...

compiled in 0.11 s at 12 links, 1.7 s at 16 and 25 s at 20, with `.`
and with `&.`, whatever call the argument is. #4925 was the same with a
lone operand, cured by rendering it after the call (operands_last). A
call that has declined two or more is now remembered by node and takes
that path from then on: it is emitted with its operands named, and they
are rendered only if it takes them. 20 links compile in 0.02 s, 200 in
1.6 s and 512 in 10 s. Past 100 links the time grows four to six times
for each doubling; a chain with literal arguments grows about four
times, on master and here.

The first emission of every call is as before. `make cident` against
fa08b100d: 6109 identical, 1 differ. In test/typed_array_assoc_nil a
call that had declined is emitted again where it takes its operands,
and their temps are numbered after the bindings instead of before.
optcarrot's C is identical.

`make scale-test` compiles test/scale/call_chain_operands.rb, forty
links in seven shapes, under its CPU-time limit; without the change it
is killed there.
