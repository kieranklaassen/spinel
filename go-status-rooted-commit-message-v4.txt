The first Process::Status stays rooted while a second `$?` is read

    system("exit 3")
    p $? == $?

printed false at SPINEL_GC_STRESS=2; CRuby prints true. A read of `$?`
builds its Process::Status (sp_last_process_status allocates), and the
`==` and `!=` templates of two statuses (builtin_ops.c) bind the first
to a C temporary and then evaluate the second: that allocation
collected the first, and its status word was read from freed memory.

The two rows now go through emit_op_pstatus_cmp. Where the second
operand is a read of `$?` and the first operand's value is one too
(the read, or a begin or `&&` whose value is one, or `||` whose left
arm is one), the template is emitted with its receiver rooted. Every
other pair gets the template as it stands: `a || $?` is one, its
value is a's wherever a holds a status.
