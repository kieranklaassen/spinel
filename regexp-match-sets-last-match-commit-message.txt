String#match and Regexp#match leave $~ at their match

sp_re_matchdata and sp_re_matchdata_at ran the engine into a caps array
of their own and handed the registers the Strings alone. The positions
(sp_re_caps) and the pattern (sp_re_last_pat) stayed those of the match
before, and $~ is built from them:

    line = "2026-10-05 x"
    line.match(/(\d+)-(\d+)/)
    p $~.to_a, $~.begin(2)

printed ["", "", ""] and 0; CRuby prints ["2026-10", "2026", "10"] and
5. After a match on another String it printed pieces of the new subject
cut at the old positions, and a named group of the new pattern raised
IndexError. $1 and $~[2] were right: they read the Strings.

Both now hand the match to sp_re_set_last_match, as gsub, sub and scan
do.
