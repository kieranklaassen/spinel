$+ is the last group that took part, past the eighth too

$+ walked the kept Strings one place off (sp_re_captures[_bri-1], from
9 down), so a ninth group answered the eighth, and a group past the
ninth was never reached:

    "abcdefghi" =~ /(a)(b)(c)(d)(e)(f)(g)(h)(i)/
    p $+

printed "h"; CRuby prints "i". With twelve groups it printed "h" where
CRuby prints the twelfth.

$+ now calls sp_re_last_paren_match. Past the ninth group it walks the
kept positions from the pattern's last group down, where the positions
are this match's own (sp_re_caps_own), and reads the first that took
part through sp_re_group; the first nine it knows by their kept
Strings, from the ninth down.

Tests: test/regexp_last_paren_match_past_eighth.rb, and
test/regexp_last_paren_match_kept_match.rb, a program left alone.
