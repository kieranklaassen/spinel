A &. tap or then on a receiver that is nil alone runs no block

    $n = 0
    nil&.then { $n += 10 }
    p $n

printed 10, and `def t; nil&.tap { $n += 10 }; end` did not build
(variable or field '_t1' declared void). The tap and then emitters
carry a nil receiver boxed, for `nil.tap { }`, and never look at the
operator, so with `&.` they bound the receiver and ran the block.

sn_nil_tap_then names the call: `&.` tap, then or yield_self with a
block on a receiver whose type is nil alone. sn_guard_pending stands
the emitters down for it and the nil arm of emit_call_safe_nav_arms
answers nil. A receiver that is more than a read was evaluated by
those emitters and still is, once: by that arm, or by
emit_iteration_stmt where the call is a statement.

A receiver that is itself a `&.` call no guard takes is left as it
was: in `none&.size&.tap { }` the receiver of `size` is dropped when
it is nil alone, which is not this change's.
