clear, slice! and bytesplice after a String mutator reach the variable

    t = +"abc"
    t.clear.concat("z")
    p t

printed "" where CRuby prints "z", and `t.concat("x").clear` left
"abcx". Pull request 7778 took the chain a value-form mutator writes
its result back through (str_bang_chain_var) past insert, prepend,
concat, replace and <<. Two things were left.

clear, force_encoding, bytesplice and append_as_bytes answer their
receiver as those five do, and the chain ended at them:
`s.bytesplice(0, 1, "Z") << "x"` lost the "x". The chain passes them
now.

clear, slice! and bytesplice change their receiver in place where it
is a variable and write nothing back, so after a link they changed the
link's value, a copy. On a chain from a String variable the chain now
runs once, for what it does, and the call is then emitted on the
variable (emit_str_chain_own_call): the chain's value is that
variable's String. A bang link that changed nothing answers nil and
the call raises NoMethodError, as before. slice! and bytesplice ran
such a receiver two to four times: `t.concat("1").bytesplice(0, 2,
"Q")` left "abcdef11". It runs once.

Both are made only where the chain's value is the variable's String.
The rest is as it was:

- a chain with a link the program gives String a method under, and a
  last call under such a name: that method need not answer its
  receiver;
- a last clear, slice! or bytesplice where a call of the chain can
  rebind the variable (read_rebound_by). Through one of the four links
  the write-back is tested when the program runs, as it is through the
  other five; where it cannot be tested, on a shared String or a boxed
  value, the chain ends at that link;
- a local read as a String out of a boxed slot (`if x.is_a?(String)`):
  its read is no lvalue, and the chain ends at the link. Through the
  other five such a chain does not build, where it was silently wrong
  before pull request 7778;
- a last clear, slice! or bytesplice on a chain from a boxed variable.
