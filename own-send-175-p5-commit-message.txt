A class's own ivar reflection methods are the ones its instances answer

    class Record
      def initialize(n) = @n = n
      def instance_variable_get(name) = "get #{name} of #{@n}"
    end
    p Record.new(3).instance_variable_get(:@n)

printed 3 where CRuby prints "get @n of 3": on a typed object the call
was lowered to a read of the field, and the class's method never ran.
An own instance_variable_set wrote the field, an own
instance_variable_defined? was answered from the layout, an own
instance_variables listed the slots, an own remove_instance_variable
answered the field, and on a Struct the set was refused.

The lowerings now stand down where the receiver's class has a method of
the name in its chain, as display's arms do: in the
inference (infer_object_call for get, set and remove, infer_call_inner
for the other two) and in the emitter (emit_object_ivar_call,
emit_call_display_ivar_arms, emit_object_call's list and its remove
arm). The call is then the ordinary call to that method, on a receiver
and on self alike.

Left as before: a boxed receiver, and a singleton method. A class with
none of the five names takes the lowering it always took, and its C is
unchanged.

Test: test/own_ivar_reflection_methods.rb (expected from CRuby 3.3.6
with --enable-frozen-string-literal).
