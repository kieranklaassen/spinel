A `&.` call on a boxed receiver runs in its place

    b = [K.new, nil, 5][0]
    p "#{$c} #{b&.bump([1, 2])}"

printed "1 [1, 2]" for a `bump` that adds one to $c; CRuby prints
"0 [1, 2]". Where the value of a `&.` call on a boxed receiver hoists
a statement, the guard was an `if` ahead of the whole statement with
the call inside it, held in a temp: the call ran before what is
written before it, in an interpolation, a multiple assignment, an
array literal.

The guard now jumps over what the value hoisted and the value stays
in the call's place, as in the typed arm and where that arm does:
where nothing the statement evaluates after the call hoists a
statement of its own. The hoisted statements still do not run for a
nil.
