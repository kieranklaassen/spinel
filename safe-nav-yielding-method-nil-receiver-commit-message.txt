A &. call of a method that yields runs nothing on a nil receiver

    class K
      def blk(a); $c += 1; yield(a); end
    end
    def mk(v) = v ? K.new : nil
    $c = 0
    o = mk(false)
    r = o&.blk([1, 2]) { |q| q.size }
    p r, $c

printed 2 and 1 where CRuby prints nil and 0: the method ran on nil,
and its block with it. A method that yields has no function of its
own; its body is spliced in place of the call, and the splices were
asked ahead of the `&.` guard and never look at the operator:
emit_inline_expr for a value, emit_inline_call for a statement. As a
statement on a receiver of several classes (`h[k]&.blk(5) { }`)
emit_poly_recv_block_dispatch spliced one arm a class, and the nil
raised NoMethodError.

emit_inline_expr declines while the call's guard is pending
(sn_guard_pending): emit_call_safe_nav_arms tests the receiver and
re-enters on the guarded temp. For the statement,
emit_iteration_stmt_sn takes the emission it goes around as a
parameter, and emit_inline_call_sn puts the two splices inside it,
only for a call that targets a yielding method or has a dispatch
candidate.

On a receiver that is not nil the call now pays the guard every
other `&.` call pays, 17 instructions a call.

As a statement, an Array or Hash literal among the arguments is
still built ahead of the guard.
