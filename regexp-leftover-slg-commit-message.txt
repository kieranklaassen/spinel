slice!(re, n) counts a negative group back and reaches the tenth

slice! with a Regexp and a group number knew the groups 1 to 9 by the
Strings the match keeps. Any other number answered nil and left the
receiver whole:

    s = +"hello"
    p s.slice!(/(e)(l)(l)/, -1), s

printed nil and "hello"; CRuby prints "l" and "helo". So did the tenth
group and beyond of a pattern that has them.

A number that is not a literal from 0 to 9 is now settled at run time: a
negative one counts back from the last group (and one past the first is
nil, as in CRuby), and a group from the tenth to the fifteenth, as far
as spans are kept, is cut out of the receiver by its span and held while
the receiver is rebuilt. A literal from 0 to 9 is emitted as before,
byte for byte.
