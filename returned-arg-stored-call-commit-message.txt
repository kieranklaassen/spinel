The returned-argument route asks a call stored into an Array or a Hash

`def id(x) = x; q << id(u); u << "z"; p q` printed ["u"] where CRuby
prints ["uz"]: the element is a copy of the String the method answers.
The route refused `v = id(u); q << v` and `id(u) << x`, the two places
sa_returned_args was asked. A call that is itself the value of a push or
of `[]=`, or an element of a literal assigned to a variable, was asked
nowhere.

Both of the route's questions are now asked there, in its sentence: the
variable passed in is mutated in place and the container is read
(refuse_string_alias_copies), or an element is mutated in place and the
variable is read (strbuf_demand_store_leaf, beside the refusal of
`a << (s << "y")`).

Not here: a constructor argument, `insert`, `concat`, a literal that is
not assigned to a variable, and --share-strings, where a local that
shares its handle through the method's parameter counts as mutated and
the form with a local between compiles. Those still hold a copy.
